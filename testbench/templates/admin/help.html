Choosing a module

Pick by the question you are asking.

  • Which of two designs is easier to use?

    Use an A/B test. Each person does the same tasks on both. You get success, time and ease, and a plain verdict.

  • Who are your participants, and what is hard for them?

    Use a survey. Put it first so other modules can use its answers, for example to show different questions to staff.

  • Where would people look for something?

    Use a tree test on your menu structure, with no visual design. Use a first-click test when the page layout matters.

  • How do people group your content?

    Use a card sort. Closed if you already have categories, open if you want theirs, hybrid for both. Aim for 15 to 40 cards.

Reading results

Every sentence in a report is produced by a fixed rule, not written by a model. The same numbers always give the same sentence.

Term What it means
Indicative A report is marked Indicative until enough people have finished. The number is set in the module’s decision rule for A/B tests, and is 8 for the other types. Below it, no report says a design won.
Intervals A range next to a number shows how far it could be from the truth. If the range crosses zero, the difference may be chance, and the report says so.
A/B verdict Each check (speed, success, ease) reads for, against or unsure, then the overall line follows the strongest pattern: all three for, a mix of for and against, all against, or not enough evidence on any check. It is never stronger than the least certain check.
Order effect Only stated as a fact, for example “Search and filters is faster in both orders.” It never claims that order did or did not drive the result — that is not something the numbers alone can show.
Drop-off A module is flagged when at least 2 people and at least 20% stopped at the same point. One person leaving is not flagged.
Card sort agreement A card has a clear home when at least 70% of people used the same category. Under 50% is “no clear home”.
First click The share of people who clicked inside the target area, and the median time to their first click.
Tree test Success across all tasks, success without backtracking, and the single weakest task by name and share.
Sign test Where a check is about direction (for example, which variant was faster for each person), a p-value from the exact two-sided test is shown alongside it.

Every sentence above is produced the same way for every study — nothing is written by hand or by a model.

Editing as YAML

For people who prefer a file.

Every module editor has an Advanced tab with the same file the form writes. Edit either. If you change the file, the form shows what it understood the next time you open it.

Every save checks the whole study first. If anything is wrong, nothing is written and the problems are listed with the file and line.
